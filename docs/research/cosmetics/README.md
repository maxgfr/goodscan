## Report

# Cosmetic ingredients of concern: EU, French and US rules, SCCS opinions and hazard lists (R2)

Research date: 30 October 2026. Scope: EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 Annexes II, III, V and VI and the CMR omnibus acts, the fragrance-allergen labelling list of Regulation (EU) 2023/1545, SCCS opinions on preservatives and UV filters, REACH restrictions on cyclosiloxanes, the French PFAS law, the IARC talc evaluation, the edlists.org endocrine-disruptor lists, California's Toxic-Free Cosmetics Act and its amendments, Washington's formaldehyde-releaser rule, and the US federal MoCRA. [M]

## Key findings

- EU law prohibits substances classified as CMR of category 1A, 1B or 2 in cosmetic products unless the exception conditions of Article 15 are met [S3].
- The CMR omnibus acts list such substances in Annex II: Regulation (EU) 2021/1902 applies from 1 March 2022 [S3], Regulation (EU) 2022/1531 from 17 December 2022 [S86], Regulation (EU) 2023/1490 from 1 December 2023 [S72], Regulation (EU) 2025/877 from 1 September 2025 [S45] and Regulation (EU) 2026/78 from 1 May 2026 [S128].
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 adds 56 fragrance allergens identified by the SCCS to the individual labelling obligation, on top of the 24 allergens already listed in Annex III entries 45 and 67 to 92 [S4].
- The REACH restriction on D4, D5 and D6 applies to all cosmetic products other than wash-off D4/D5 products after 6 June 2027, not 6 June 2026 as one secondary source states [S21][S28].
- France bans cosmetic products containing PFAS from 1 January 2026 [S127], and California has banned intentionally added PFAS in cosmetics since 1 January 2025 [S36].
- IARC classified talc as probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A) in July 2024 [S112].

## 1. EU framework and the CMR omnibus regulations

Article 15 of Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 provides that substances classified as CMR of category 1A, 1B or 2 under Part 3 of Annex VI to Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 are prohibited in cosmetic products [S3]. To ensure legal certainty, the Commission includes all CMR substances in the Annex II list of prohibited substances and deletes them where relevant from Annexes III to VI [S3].

Regulation (EU) 2021/1902 covers substances classified as CMR by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/1182 and applies from 1 March 2022 [S3]. It moved Zinc Pyrithione, classified CMR category 1B, to Annex II even though the SCCS considered it safe as an anti-dandruff agent in rinse-off hair products up to 1 %, because the absence of suitable alternatives had not been established [S3]. The same act added 2-(4-tert-butylbenzyl) propionaldehyde (lilial, CAS 80-54-6) to Annex II as entry 1666 and Pyrithione zinc as entry 1670 [S3].

Regulation (EU) 2022/1531 covers substances classified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/849 from 17 December 2022 [S86]. It placed Methyl Salicylate, a CMR category 2 substance for which an exception was requested, in the Annex III list of restricted substances instead of Annex II [S86].

Regulation (EU) 2023/1490 covers substances classified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/692 and applies from 1 December 2023 [S72]. Its Annex II additions include Benzophenone, trimethylolpropane triacrylate, Pentetic Acid and Pentasodium Pentetate [S72].

Regulation (EU) 2025/877 covers substances classified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/197 and applies from 1 September 2025 [S45]. It moved Trimethylbenzoyl Diphenylphosphine Oxide (TPO), classified CMR category 1B (toxic for reproduction), from Annex III entry 311 to Annex II [S45]. The Commission's question-and-answer page states that from 1 September 2025 both placing and making available on the market of cosmetic products containing TPO are prohibited [S18]. The same page states that the Cosmetics Regulation contains no sell-through or use-up provision for CMR category 1A or 1B substances [S18].

Regulation (EU) 2026/78 covers substances classified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2564 and applies from 1 May 2026 [S128]. It adds Hexyl Salicylate to the Annex III restricted list, keeps o-Phenylphenol as an Annex V preservative with Sodium o-Phenylphenate added, and restricts silver according to particle size [S128].

Regulation (EU) No 358/2014 banned isopropyl-, isobutyl-, phenyl-, benzyl- and pentylparaben because limited or no safety information was submitted [S126]. Regulation (EU) 2017/1410 banned HICC, atranol and chloroatranol, the fragrance allergens that caused the highest number of contact allergy cases in past years [S124]. Regulation (EU) 2024/996 restricted Vitamin A, Alpha-Arbutin, Arbutin and substances with potential endocrine disrupting properties and banned 4-Methylbenzylidene Camphor [S92].

## 2. Fragrance allergens: Regulation (EU) 2023/1545

Before the amendment, 24 fragrance allergens listed in Annex III entries 45 and 67 to 92 had to be mentioned in the list of ingredients [S4]. The SCCS opinion of 26-27 June 2012 confirmed that those allergens are still relevant and identified 56 additional fragrance allergens that have clearly caused allergies in humans [S4]. Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 therefore introduces individual labelling of those allergens when their concentration exceeds 0,001 % in leave-on products and 0,01 % in rinse-off products [S4].

The regulation replaces 17 Annex III entries, deletes 10 entries and adds 45 entries [S12]. For the new entries, non-compliant cosmetic products may be placed on the Union market until 31 July 2026 and made available on the Union market until 31 July 2028 [S4]. The regulation sets transition periods of 3 and 5 years respectively [S4]. Fragrance substances such as prehaptens and prohaptens that turn into known contact allergens through air oxidation or bioactivation are treated as equivalent to fragrance allergens [S4].

The SCCS opinion behind the list is registered as SCCS/1459/11 and dated 26-27 June 2012 [S4]. Ireland's regulator HPRA restates the 31 July 2026 and 31 July 2028 deadlines [S25].

## 3. SCCS opinions and EU rules on preservatives

Parabens. The SCCS opinion SCCS/1514/13 found that adequate evidence had not been provided for the safe use of propyl- or butylparaben in cosmetics [S49]. Regulation (EU) No 1004/2014 then capped butyl- and propylparaben at 0,14 % (sum, as acid) and banned them in leave-on products for the nappy area of children under three, from 16 April 2015 [S110]. The later opinion SCCS/1623/20 assessed propylparaben as a preservative up to 0.14 % [S69]. SCCS/1652/23 concluded that methylparaben is safe as a preservative at up to 0.4 % (expressed as acid) [S129].

Isothiazolinones. Regulation (EU) No 1003/2014 restricted the Methylchloroisothiazolinone (and) Methylisothiazolinone mixture to rinse-off products at 0,0015 % [S57]. Regulation (EU) 2016/1198 banned Methylisothiazolinone in leave-on products, including wet wipes, from 12 February 2017 [S114]. Regulation (EU) 2017/1224 lowered it to 0,0015 % in rinse-off products, following the SCCS opinion of 15 December 2015 [S20]. That opinion, SCCS/1557/15, found no safe concentration of MI for leave-on products [S29].

Triclosan and triclocarban. The SCCS advice SCCS/1643/22 found triclosan safe in toothpaste at 0.3 % when used individually but not safe in combinations for children below 3 years old [S10]. Regulation (EU) 2024/996 limits Triclosan to 0,3 % in listed product types and bars its use in toothpaste for children under 3 years [S92].

Formaldehyde and formaldehyde releasers. Regulation (EU) 2019/831 moved Formaldehyde, Paraformaldehyde, Methylene Glycol and Quaternium-15 into Annex II [S82]. The SCCS advised that the former 0.05 % threshold for labelling formaldehyde releasers did not sufficiently protect consumers sensitised to formaldehyde [S46]. Regulation (EU) 2022/1181 therefore requires the warning 'releases formaldehyde' when total released formaldehyde exceeds 0,001 % (10 ppm) [S125]. A UK SAG-CS opinion lists DMDM hydantoin, imidazolidinyl urea, diazolidinyl urea and bronopol among formaldehyde releasers permitted in Annex V [S90]. An October 2023 OCCL market surveillance study found DMDM hydantoin, diazolidinyl urea and imidazolidinyl urea to be the most frequently used releasers in the products tested [S105].

## 4. UV filters and endocrine disruptors

Regulation (EU) 2022/1176 restricted Benzophenone-3 and Octocrylene after a 2019 call for data on their potential endocrine disrupting properties [S115]. Regulation (EU) 2022/2195 restricted Homosalate to face products at 7,34 % after the SCCS advice of 2 December 2021 [S87][S70]. The earlier opinion SCCS/1622/20 had found homosalate not safe as a UV filter at up to 10 % [S5]. The SCCS could not conclude on the safety of 4-MBC and found sufficient evidence that it may act as an endocrine disruptor [S61].

The edlists.org website was launched in June 2020 by national authorities of Belgium, Denmark, France, the Netherlands and Sweden, and Spain joined in February 2021 [S38]. Its List I holds substances identified as endocrine disruptors under EU plant protection, biocide or REACH legislation [S50]. At launch List I held 19 substances, List II 84 substances under investigation and List III 9 substances [S11]. As of April 2022 no compound was identified or regulated for endocrine disrupting properties under the Cosmetic Products Regulation itself [S50]. List II contains substances under evaluation in an EU legislative process, such as Oxybenzone (BP-3) and BHT [S103]. List III contains substances that a participating national authority considers endocrine disruptors, such as methylparaben and propylparaben [S109]. In this database only List I substances receive the 'endocrine' category. [M]

## 5. Siloxanes, PFAS and talc

Regulation (EU) 2018/35 restricted D4 and D5 in wash-off cosmetic products as REACH Annex XVII entry 70 [S68]. Regulation (EU) 2024/1328 replaced entry 70 and added D6 [S21]. Under the new entry, D4 and D5 in wash-off cosmetics have been limited since 31 January 2020, and the 0,1 % limit applies to all other cosmetic products after 6 June 2027 [S21]. D4 was identified as PBT, while D5 and D6 were identified as vPvB substances [S21].

Article L524-1 of the French Environment Code bans, from 1 January 2026, the manufacture, import, export and placing on the market of any cosmetic product containing PFAS [S127]. Decree No 2025-1376 defines PFAS as any substance with at least one fully fluorinated methyl (CF3-) or methylene (-CF2-) carbon atom [S95]. The decree sets residual thresholds of 25 ppb for any targeted PFAS, 250 ppb for their sum and 50 ppm for PFAS including polymers [S95]. It also allows a 12-month sell-off for products made before 1 January 2026 [S95]. The Breast Cancer Prevention Partners PFAS list names INCI ingredients such as PTFE, C9-15 Fluoroalcohol Phosphate, Perfluorooctyl Triethoxysilane, Perfluorononyl Dimethicone and Polyperfluoromethylisopropyl Ether [S107].

IARC's Working Group classified talc as probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A), citing limited evidence for ovarian cancer in humans, sufficient evidence in experimental animals and strong mechanistic evidence [S112]. The evaluation was announced in a press release dated 5 July 2024 [S112]. The full monograph is indexed as IARC Monographs volume 136 on talc and acrylonitrile [S121].

## 6. United States

California's AB 2762 was chaptered as Chapter 314 and added Section 108980 to the Health and Safety Code [S58]. From 1 January 2025, Section 108980(a) bars cosmetics containing 24 intentionally added ingredients, including dibutyl and diethylhexyl phthalate, formaldehyde, paraformaldehyde, methylene glycol, quaternium-15, mercury, isobutyl- and isopropylparaben, m- and o-phenylenediamine and 13 PFAS [S116]. AB 496 (Chapter 441) adds a second list of banned ingredients from 1 January 2027 [S116]. The 2027 list includes lily aldehyde, cyclotetrasiloxane, phytonadione and boron substances [S116]. AB 2771 bans cosmetic products containing intentionally added PFAS from 1 January 2025 [S36]. Technically unavoidable trace quantities from impurities, manufacturing, storage or packaging do not make a product non-compliant [S116].

Washington bans 25 listed formaldehyde releasers intentionally added to cosmetics from 1 January 2027 [S78].

The federal Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 requires responsible persons to report serious adverse events to FDA within 15 business days [S41]. Manufacturers and processors must register their facilities with FDA and renew every two years [S41]. Each marketed product must be listed with FDA, with updates provided annually [S41]. FDA can order a mandatory recall when a responsible person refuses to recall voluntarily [S41]. MoCRA requires FDA to issue rules on good manufacturing practice, fragrance allergen labelling and standardized asbestos testing of talc-containing cosmetics [S41]. FDA published its MoCRA report on PFAS in cosmetic products on 29 December 2025 [S41]. A trade press report says FDA plans a proposed rule on formaldehyde in hair smoothing products in November 2026 [S44]. FDA lists mercury and methylene chloride among prohibited or restricted cosmetic ingredients [S106].

## 7. Contradictions and caveats

- ADSL describes zinc pyrithione and butylphenyl methylpropional as Annex II entries 1666 and 1670 respectively [S8], but the EUR-Lex text lists 2-(4-tert-butylbenzyl) propionaldehyde at 1666 and Pyrithione zinc at 1670 [S3].
- CosmeticOBS dates the D4/D5/D6 cosmetic restriction to 6 June 2026 with a leave-on derogation to 2027 [S28], whereas the official text applies the limit to all cosmetics other than wash-off D4/D5 after 6 June 2027 [S21].
- A secondary source describes lilial as classified Repr. 2 under CLP [S24]; this database therefore does not tag lilial as CMR 1A/1B.
