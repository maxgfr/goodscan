## Report

# US federal and state laws restricting food additives (2023–2026)

## TL;DR

- Federally, FDA has revoked two additive authorizations outright: brominated vegetable oil (rule effective August 2, 2024, compliance one year later) [S27][S7] and FD&C Red No. 3 in food and ingested drugs (deadlines January 15, 2027 and January 18, 2028) [S40][S109].
- FDA also revoked the Orange B listing (order effective September 8, 2026) and has only proposed revoking Citrus Red No. 2 [S132][S45][S130].
- The April 2025 plan to remove the six other petroleum-based dyes is voluntary: no rulemaking has been initiated and the dyes remain federally legal [S25][S55].
- Statewide sales bans exist in California (four additives from January 1, 2027) [S98], Arkansas (potassium bromate and propylparaben from January 1, 2028) [S144], and West Virginia (seven dyes plus BHA and propylparaben from January 1, 2028, currently enjoined) [S13][S24].
- Texas requires a warning label on foods with any of 44 listed ingredients for labels from January 1, 2027, but enforcement is preliminarily enjoined against the plaintiff associations [S126][S79]; Louisiana requires a QR-code disclosure from December 31, 2028 [S141][S135].
- At least thirteen states have enacted school-meal restrictions, with start dates from August 1, 2025 to the 2028–29 school year [S25].

## What it is

This report covers binding US rules that restrict specific food additives: FDA revocations of food-additive or color-additive authorizations, state laws banning additives in all food sold in a state, state laws restricting additives in school food, and state labeling mandates [S25].
It also covers federal announcements that are not binding, which are flagged as such [S50][S25].

## How it works / key concepts

- Color additives require FDA pre-market approval, and the Delaney Clause bars FDA from authorizing a color additive found to induce cancer in humans or animals [S40].
- FD&C Red No. 3 may still appear in food or drug products on the market past the effective date if they were manufactured before it [S40].
- An FDA final order on a color additive can be stayed by the filing of objections, which is what happened to the Red No. 3 order [S109][S25].
- State laws work through three channels: sales bans enforced by state officials [S98][S144], school-food rules [S25], and label or disclosure mandates [S126][S117].

## History & evolution

### Federal (FDA)

**Brominated vegetable oil (BVO).**
- BVO was authorized as a stabilizer for fruit flavoring in beverages at no more than 15 parts per million [S27].
- FDA proposed revoking the regulation on November 2, 2023, and issued the final rule on July 3, 2024 [S27].
- The final rule removes 21 CFR 180.30 [S9].
- The rule is effective August 2, 2024, and the compliance date is one year after the effective date [S27].
- The compliance date is August 2, 2025, and the revocation applies to alcohol beverages too [S7].

**FD&C Red No. 3 (erythrosine).**
- FDA revoked the authorization for FD&C Red No. 3 in food and ingested drugs based on the Delaney Clause [S40][S51].
- Manufacturers have until January 15, 2027 (food) or January 18, 2028 (ingested drugs) to reformulate [S40].
- FDA said the cancer finding comes from male rats and that there is no evidence it causes cancer in humans [S40].
- Objections filed for Micro-Tracers, Inc. stayed the order as of February 18, 2025 [S25].
- On August 5, 2026 FDA denied the objections, ended the administrative stay and confirmed the effective dates of January 15, 2027 and January 18, 2028 [S109].
- In a July 14, 2025 letter, FDA encouraged manufacturers to stop using FD&C Red No. 3 in foods as soon as practicable, ahead of the binding deadline [S102].

**Orange B and Citrus Red No. 2.**
- The April 22, 2025 HHS/FDA announcement said FDA would start the process to revoke authorization for Citrus Red No. 2 and Orange B [S50].
- On July 22, 2026 FDA issued a final order revoking Orange B and proposed revoking Citrus Red No. 2 [S45].
- The Orange B order is effective September 8, 2026 [S132].
- FDA based the Orange B action on abandonment, noting that Orange B has not been certified for use in food marketed in the United States since 1978 [S132].
- Citrus Red No. 2 has been authorized since 1959 for coloring the skins of mature oranges, and comments on its proposed revocation closed August 24, 2026 [S45].
- FDA proposed that a final Citrus Red No. 2 order take effect 90 days after publication [S130].
- No final Citrus Red No. 2 order had been published as of September 15, 2026 [S25].

**The 2025 synthetic-dye phase-out plan.**
- On April 22, 2025 HHS and FDA said they would work with industry to eliminate FD&C Green No. 3, Red No. 40, Yellow No. 5, Yellow No. 6, Blue No. 1 and Blue No. 2 from the food supply by the end of next year [S50].
- The same announcement asked companies to remove FD&C Red No. 3 sooner than the 2027-2028 deadline [S50].
- The phase-out of the six dyes is voluntary: no rulemaking has been initiated, the dyes remain federally legal, and adherence rests on company pledges [S25].
- Consumer Reports likewise reports that FDA has not banned the dyes and instead called on manufacturers to phase them out voluntarily [S55].
- FDA tracks industry pledges on a public webpage [S26].
- On February 5, 2026 FDA said it would use enforcement discretion so that products without petroleum-based colors can claim "no artificial colors" [S61].

**Other federal actions through 2026.**
- FDA published a proposed rule that would eliminate the self-affirmed GRAS pathway and require FDA notification of GRAS conclusions [S118].
- The mandatory GRAS-notification proposal was published August 11, 2026, with comments due December 9, 2026 [S25].
- FDA reviews of BHA, BHT and ADA are underway under its post-market assessment program [S25].

### States

**California.**
- AB 418, the California Food Safety Act, was chaptered on October 7, 2023 as Chapter 328, Statutes of 2023 [S98].
- Commencing January 1, 2027, it bars manufacturing, selling, delivering, distributing, holding or offering for sale any food product containing brominated vegetable oil, potassium bromate, propylparaben or Red dye 3 [S98].
- Violations carry a civil penalty of up to $5,000 for a first violation and up to $10,000 for each later one [S98].
- Titanium dioxide was removed from the bill by amendment [S53].
- AB 2316, the California School Food Safety Act, was chaptered on September 28, 2024 as Chapter 914, Statutes of 2024 [S103].
- Beginning December 31, 2027, AB 2316 bars Blue 1, Blue 2, Green 3, Red 40, Yellow 5 and Yellow 6 from food offered or sold to K–12 students in public schools, with exceptions for fundraising sales [S103].
- AB 1264, the Real Food, Healthy Kids Act of October 8, 2025, sets up a phase-out of ultraprocessed foods in schools from the 2029–30 through the 2035–36 school years [S25].

**West Virginia.**
- Governor Morrisey signed HB 2354 on March 24, 2025 [S58][S18].
- Effective August 1, 2025, Red Dye No. 3 and No. 40, and the other listed dyes, may not be an ingredient in any meal served in a school nutrition program [S13].
- From January 1, 2028, food is deemed adulterated if it contains butylated hydroxyanisole, propylparaben, FD&C Blue No. 1, Blue No. 2, Green No. 3, Red No. 3, Red No. 40, Yellow No. 5 or Yellow No. 6 [S13].
- On December 23, 2025 a federal judge preliminarily enjoined the statewide provisions, and the injunction does not cover the school-meal provision [S24][S106].
- The state's appeal is fully briefed at the Fourth Circuit and was tentatively calendared for argument during the December 8–11, 2026 session [S25].

**Arkansas.**
- Act 622 of 2025 (SB 9) bars manufacturing, selling or offering for sale a food product containing potassium bromate or propylparaben [S144].
- Act 622 is effective on and after January 1, 2028, carries civil penalties of up to $5,000 and $10,000, and was approved 4/16/25 [S144].

**Texas.**
- Governor Abbott signed SB 25 on June 22, 2025 [S125].
- SB 25 requires a warning label on food containing any of 44 listed ingredients, reading "WARNING: This product contains an ingredient that is not recommended for human consumption by the appropriate authority in Australia, Canada, the European Union, or the United Kingdom." [S126]
- The listed ingredients include azodicarbonamide, BHA, BHT, Blue 1, Blue 2, Citrus red 2, Green 3, potassium bromate, propylparaben, Red 3, Red 40, titanium dioxide, Yellow 5 and Yellow 6 [S126].
- The warning requirement applies only to a food product label developed or copyrighted on or after January 1, 2027 [S126].
- On February 11, 2026 a federal court enjoined the Attorney General from enforcing the warning requirement against the four plaintiff trade associations and their members [S79].
- Texas DSHS finalized implementing rules on February 20, 2026, and Paxton appealed on March 5, 2026 [S96].
- Texas SB 314 bars BVO, potassium bromate, propylparaben, azodicarbonamide, BHA, Red 3, Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1, Blue 2, Green 3, Orange B, INS No. 150c and 150d ammonia caramels and titanium dioxide, among others, from free or reduced-price school meals [S142].
- SB 314 applies beginning with the 2026-2027 school year, to contracts entered into or renewed on or after September 1, 2026 [S142].
- SB 314 was signed on May 27, 2025 [S120].

**Louisiana.**
- SB 14 became law on June 25, 2025 [S119].
- SB 14 bars public schools and state-funded nonpublic schools from serving food containing Blue 1, Blue 2, Green 3, Red 3, Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, azodicarbonamide, BHA, BHT, potassium bromate, propylparaben, acesulfame potassium, aspartame or sucralose [S117].
- The school provision takes effect beginning with the 2028-2029 school year [S117].
- SB 14 also requires a QR code and a notice on labels of foods containing listed ingredients [S117].
- The QR-code list includes aspartame, azodicarbonamide, BHA, BHT, Blue dye 1, Blue dye 2, Citrus red dye 2, Green dye 3, potassium bromate, propylparaben, Red dye 3, Red dye 40, sucralose, Yellow dye 5 and Yellow dye 6 [S117].
- Act 735 of 2026 (SB 57) removed acesulfame potassium from both lists and moved the labeling effective date from Jan. 1, 2028 to Dec. 31, 2028 [S135][S141].

**Other school-meal laws.**
- Arizona HB 2164 bars schools in federally funded meal programs from serving or selling food with potassium bromate, propylparaben, titanium dioxide, brominated vegetable oil, Yellow dye 5 or 6, Blue dye 1 or 2, Green dye 3, or Red dye 3 or 40, beginning in the 2026-2027 school year [S43].
- Arizona HB 2164 was approved by the Governor on April 14, 2025 [S43].
- Utah HB 402 bars public schools from serving food with potassium bromate, propylparaben or seven FD&C dyes beginning in the 2026-2027 school year, and exempts districts of 5,500 students or fewer [S38].
- Utah HB 402 was signed on March 27 [S8].
- Virginia's Code § 22.1-207.4:3, effective July 1, 2027, bars school meals and competitive foods containing FD&C Blue No. 1, Blue No. 2, Green No. 3, Red No. 3, Red No. 40, Yellow No. 5 or Yellow No. 6 [S147].
- Governor Youngkin signed Virginia HB 1910 and SB 1289 on March 21 [S22].
- Delaware SB 69 bars schools from selling or serving breakfast, lunch or competitive food containing Red dye 40, implemented beginning July 1, 2026 [S59].
- Delaware SB 69 was signed by Governor Meyer on May 22 [S87].
- Tennessee's SB 2423 bars Red 40, Red 3, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1, Blue 2 and Green 3 in school nutrition programs on or after August 1, 2027, expanding a 2025 Red 40 ban [S16].
- Nebraska LB 940, signed April 14, 2026, bars Blue No. 1, Blue No. 2, Green No. 3, Red No. 40, Yellow No. 5 and Yellow No. 6 from school meals from August 1, 2027 [S16].
- Iowa HF 2676 bans six food dyes and two additives from most food served at K-12 schools [S136][S113].
- Iowa's list includes potassium bromate and propylparaben, and the provision applies to school years beginning on or after July 1, 2027 [S113].
- Iowa's six dyes are Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1, Blue 2 and Green 3 [S25].
- Alaska SB 187 became law without the governor's signature on June 18, 2026 as Chapter 17 SLA 26, effective 1/1/28 [S133].
- Alaska SB 187 restricts Red Dye No. 3, Red Dye No. 40, Yellow Dye No. 5, Yellow Dye No. 6, Blue Dye No. 1, Blue Dye No. 2 and Green Dye No. 3 in school breakfast or lunch [S113].
- New Hampshire SB 577, signed July 2, 2026, bars Red Dye No. 3, Citrus Red No. 2 and Orange B in school breakfast or lunch from July 1, 2028, and only encourages schools to avoid six other dyes [S113].

## Current state (today)

- In force now: the BVO revocation [S7], the Orange B revocation [S25], and school-meal rules in West Virginia, Delaware, Utah, Arizona and Texas [S25].
- Coming in 2027: California AB 418 on January 1, 2027 and FDA's Red No. 3 food deadline on January 15, 2027 [S25].
- Federally, Red 40 and the other five petroleum-based dyes are not banned [S25].
- New York's Food Safety and Chemical Disclosure Act passed both chambers but had not been delivered to or signed by the Governor as of September 15, 2026 [S25].
- Michigan HB 5069 is pending, not law [S25].

## Notable variants / approaches

- Statewide sales bans: California [S98], Arkansas [S144] and West Virginia [S13].
- Label mandates: a fixed warning text in Texas [S126] versus a QR-code notice in Louisiana [S117].
- School-only rules vary in reach: Texas SB 314 covers only free or reduced-price meals [S142], while Virginia also covers competitive foods [S147].

## Controversies & open debates

- The West Virginia court found the "poisonous and injurious" standard likely unconstitutionally vague [S25].
- The Texas court found the SB 25 warning likely violates the First Amendment by compelling speech [S79].
- FDA revoked Red No. 3 under the Delaney Clause while saying there is no evidence it causes cancer in humans [S40].

## Practical implications

- Products sold in California must be free of BVO, potassium bromate, propylparaben and Red dye 3 from January 1, 2027 [S98].
- The two January 2027 deadlines work as national reformulation dates in practice [S25].
